PART 1 - EVIDENCE FROM OTHER SYSTEMS (Chapter 2)
============================================================
These two are the important ones. They defend claims a
marker is most likely to challenge.


------------------------------------------------------------
1. evidence_sunway.png            -> Figure 2.2, section 2.3
------------------------------------------------------------
PROVES: institutions enforce booking policy by hand, with
        policy text and staff time instead of software.

STEPS
 1. Open https://sunway.libcal.com/reserve/gpp
 2. Do not log in. You do not need to.
 3. Scroll until you can see BOTH of these lines together,
    they sit just above the availability calendar:

      "Group size, minimum 3 to maximum of 10 persons
       (For POD 1-5) and minimum 3 to maximum of 6 persons
       (For POD 6-11)."

      "Users must show up at the Service Counter (Ground
       Level) within the first 15 minutes of the booked
       timeslot. If not, the bookings will be considered
       forfeited and may be cancelled."

 4. Make sure the Sunway library header is visible at the
    top, so the source is obvious from the image itself.
 5. IMPORTANT - get the date into the shot. Either:
      - leave the browser clock/taskbar visible, or
      - include part of the availability calendar, which
        shows the current date range
    Your caption says "captured August 2026" and the image
    should back that up.
 6. Capture:  Win + Shift + S  -> drag  -> paste into Paint
    -> save as PNG
 7. Save as:  report/figs/evidence_sunway.png

WHY THE DATE MATTERS
 It is a live page. If Sunway changes it before your viva,
 a dated screenshot is still valid evidence. An undated one
 is not.


------------------------------------------------------------
2. evidence_midas.png             -> Figure 2.1, section 2.3
------------------------------------------------------------
PROVES: alternatives exist in one commercial product, but
        they are not ranked and the substitutions are
        configured by hand.

STEPS
 1. Open https://mid.as/online-demo
    (If the demo will not let you book, use the free trial
     at https://mid.as/free-trial instead.)
 2. Find the booking or availability screen.
 3. Pick a room and a time that is ALREADY TAKEN. If nothing
    is taken, make a booking first, then try to book the
    same slot again.
 4. Submit it. MIDAS should respond with alternatives -
    other times the same day, the same time on nearby days,
    or other rooms.
 5. Capture that list of alternatives.

 6. BONUS SHOT, worth the extra two minutes:
    Look for the admin screen where substitute venues are
    specified - it is under venue settings, described on
    https://mid.as/features/booking-alternatives
    A shot of that screen shows the substitutions are
    maintained BY HAND, which is exactly your argument.
    If you get it, put both images side by side in one
    picture before saving.

 7. Save as:  report/figs/evidence_midas.png

WHAT TO LOOK FOR WHILE YOU ARE IN THERE
 - Are the alternatives in any order of quality, or just
   listed?
 - Does anything mention how many people are in the group?
 - Does it remember what you chose last time?
 The answer to all three is no. That is your objective 3.

PART 2 - YOUR OWN SYSTEM (Chapter 3)
============================================================
Run the app first:   npm run dev
Then log in as each role.

Before you start: seed a bit of realistic data. Empty
dashboards look unfinished. A few facilities, a handful of
bookings across different days, one or two no-shows.

3. gui_student.png                       -> Figure 3.6
------------------------------------------------------------
SHOWS: the student dashboard.

 1. Log in as a student.
 2. Land on the dashboard with the summary strip visible
    (upcoming, awaiting approval, free now).
 3. Have at least two bookings in "My bookings" so the
    section is not empty.
 4. Capture the whole window, not just part of it.

------------------------------------------------------------
4. gui_manager.png                       -> Figure 3.7
------------------------------------------------------------
SHOWS: the facility manager dashboard, scoped to their
       assigned facilities only.

 1. Log in as a facility manager.
 2. Open the approval queue with at least two pending
    requests waiting.
 3. If the assigned-facility name is visible anywhere,
    make sure it is in the shot - it demonstrates scoping.
 4. Save as: report/figs/gui_manager.png


------------------------------------------------------------
5. gui_admin.png                         -> Figure 3.8
------------------------------------------------------------
SHOWS: the administrator dashboard and facility
       configuration.

 1. Log in as administrator.
 2. Open the Facilities tab, showing a facility TYPE with
    its rules, and the rooms underneath it.
 3. This is your two-level model on screen - the single
    most important thing in Chapter 3. Make sure both
    levels are visible in one shot.
 4. Save as: report/figs/gui_admin.png


------------------------------------------------------------
6. gui_recommend.png                     -> Figure 3.9
------------------------------------------------------------
SHOWS: the AI recommendation. Objective 3.

 1. Log in as a student.
 2. Pick a facility and request a time you know is ALREADY
    BOOKED. If nothing is booked, book something as another
    user first.
 3. The system should refuse and offer ranked alternatives.
 4. THE CRITICAL PART: the reason text must be visible on
    each suggestion - "close to your preferred time",
    "good fit for your group size", "off-peak, likely
    quieter". That text is what separates your recommender
    from MIDAS. Do not crop it out.
 5. Save as: report/figs/gui_recommend.png


------------------------------------------------------------
7. gui_chatbot.png                       -> Figure 3.10
------------------------------------------------------------
SHOWS: the grounded assistant. Objective 4.

 1. Log in as a student and open the chat widget.
 2. Ask something answerable from live data, for example:
      "Is the badminton court free tomorrow afternoon?"
      "How long can I book a discussion room for?"
 3. Wait for the answer and capture the question AND the
    answer together.
 4. Save as: report/figs/gui_chatbot.png

 OPTIONAL SECOND SHOT, strong for the viva:
 Ask it something outside its four question families, for
 example "what is the wifi password?", and capture it
 declining. That demonstrates the scope limit is real. Keep
 it on your laptop for the demo even if it does not go in
 the report.
