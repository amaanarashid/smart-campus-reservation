------------------------------------------------------------
NEW SLIDE 9
Title:   The eleven papers, and what each one gave the project
Kicker:  LITERATURE REVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------

Intro line:
  Reviewed paper by paper. Problem, method, outcomes, gaps
  for each. Grouped by what they contribute.

GROUP A - CAMPUS BOOKING SYSTEMS (the baseline to beat)

  Venkatesh et al. (2026), IJERT
    Web platform, request and approve. Rules fixed in code;
    conflicts checked in the application.
    -> Establishes the baseline workflow.

  Antony et al. (2026), IJOEST
    Node/Mongo, roles, calendar, analytics. Most complete
    system reviewed.
    -> Lists AI availability prediction as future work.
       Evidence the gap is recognised, not invented.

  Artes et al. (2024), ACM MSIE
    Service-blueprint methodology, user-centred.
    -> A proposal only. Never implemented, so it never had
       to solve the concurrency problem.

  Hwang, Jung and Lee (2022), JPSP
    Database design, real-time via Firestore.
    -> The argument that conflict prevention belongs in the
       data layer. Directly behind objective 2.

  Lalitha et al. (2022), IEEE ICCST
    PHP/MySQL event-hall booking.
    -> One facility type, one fixed rule set. The floor.


------------------------------------------------------------
NEW SLIDE 10
Title:   What the AI literature settles
Kicker:  LITERATURE REVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------

GROUP B - RECOMMENDATION (2 papers)

  Roy and Dutta (2022), Journal of Big Data
    Systematic review. Cold start, sparsity, scalability.
    -> THE key paper. Collaborative filtering fails in
       exactly my conditions: seven facilities, no history.
       This is why objective 3 is scored, not trained.

  Valencia-Arias et al. (2024), Intelligent Systems with
  Applications
    Bibliometric. Field moving to context-awareness and
    explainability.
    -> Why every suggestion carries its reason.

GROUP C - CHATBOTS IN HIGHER EDUCATION (4 papers)

  Okonkwo and Ade-Ibijola (2021), Computers and Education: AI
    Systematic review. Administrative queries are the
    strongest use case; incorrect answers the core risk.
    -> Why the assistant can only phrase retrieved rows.

  Rahim et al. (2022), Sustainability
    302 Malaysian HEI students, UTAUT2, PLS-SEM-ANN.
    -> Trust drives adoption. Closest evidence to my actual
       user population.

  Essel et al. (2022), IJETHE
    68 students, pretest-posttest, measured learning gain.
    -> The template for my Phase 2 evaluation design.

  Dempere et al. (2023), Frontiers in Education
    Risk review: accuracy, privacy, integrity.
    -> Why the assistant is deliberately narrow.

Closing line:
  Five papers set the baseline. Two decide the technique.
  Four set the accuracy and trust requirements.
  All eleven verified against Crossref or the publisher.
